How can someone acquire Montenegrin citizenship by descent?

Article 5 links a child’s citizenship by descent to the parents’ citizenship at birth and other stated circumstances. Article 6(1)(1) separately concerns a child born abroad; article 6(1)(2) concerns an adult before their 23rd birthday with one Montenegrin citizen parent and one foreign-citizen parent, who does not have the other parent’s citizenship and applies for registry entry before turning 23. Article 6(1)(2) states no birthplace condition and does not say the parent had to be a Montenegrin citizen at the applicant’s birth. A Montenegrin grandparent alone is not listed as an automatic route under articles 5–6.

Can marriage to a Montenegrin citizen qualify someone for citizenship?

Yes. Article 11 permits admission on application after at least three years of marriage to a Montenegrin citizen and at least five years of lawful, continuous residence in Montenegro before applying, subject to specified article 8 conditions. Marriage does not confer citizenship automatically. Since 19 August 2026, article 11(1)–(2) also applies by analogy to a person who concluded a same-sex life partnership with a Montenegrin citizen under the law governing such partnerships.

How does naturalization for Montenegrin citizenship work?

Ordinary admission under article 8 requires an application, adulthood, generally release from another citizenship (subject to statutory exceptions), ten years of lawful continuous residence before applying, housing and steady income, criminal-record requirements, basic Montenegrin language, no security impediment and settled obligations. Articles 10–11 provide distinct routes. Under article 27, the competent authority decides.

What are the requirements for Montenegrin citizenship?

Montenegrin citizenship has different legal routes: descent and birth under limited conditions, admission on application, and specific rules for spouses or same-sex life partners. Article 8 provides the ordinary admission route; Article 11, as amended with effect from 19 August 2026, also covers a same-sex life partnership concluded under the special law. No single checklist decides every applicant's eligibility.

When do documents for use in Montenegro need an apostille?

The 1961 Hague Convention applies to specified public documents between states for which it is in force. Under Article 3, an apostille replaces consular legalisation to authenticate signature, capacity and seal where authentication is required; law or treaty may remove even that formality.

Does Montenegro allow dual citizenship?

The law does not support a blanket yes or no. Article 2 addresses how someone who already has two citizenships is treated before Montenegrin authorities; ordinary admission under article 8 generally requires release from another citizenship, with stated exceptions. Article 18(2) permits dual citizenship under a reciprocal international agreement. Article 24(1)(1) provides for loss of Montenegrin citizenship when an adult voluntarily acquires another nationality, except for the specified treaty route. The constitutional implementation law protects those who already held both on 3 June 2006.

How does Montenegro tax foreign-source income?

A Montenegro tax resident is within Article 4(1) for income earned both inside and outside Montenegro. A non-resident is within Article 4(2) for income earned in Montenegro [1]. Foreign tax may be creditable under Article 44, limited to the Montenegro tax on that income, and a tax treaty can take priority under Article 45 [1].

How is tax residence determined in Montenegro?

Under Article 3, an individual is a Montenegro tax resident if they have domicile or a centre of business and vital interests in Montenegro, or stay there for more than 183 days in the tax year. The provision also covers a person sent abroad for work for a Montenegro resident or international organisation [1].
