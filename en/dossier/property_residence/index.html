I

What is the answer?

Conditional statutory ground

Property ownership is a statutory ground for applying for temporary residence, but buying a property does not itself grant a permit. Articles 38(1)(8), 43 and 56 set separate conditions.[1]–[3]

This is a general source check, not a decision on a person's permit.
II

What supports the answer?

Article 24 of the enacted amendment 3/2026 changes Article 56 and states the EUR 150,000 tax-assessment base. The Gazette confirms when it took effect. The government-hosted 2026 consolidation incorporates this amendment and 33/2026.

IV

What remains unknown?

  • The buyer's nationality, ownership share, property category, tax valuation and other Article 43 conditions are unknown.
  • The currently accepted municipal valuation document, filing steps and any later amendments need a fresh check for a real application.
  • Existing permit holders can be affected by the transitional renewal rule in Article 217b; that person's history was not supplied.
V

What should you check before acting?

Ownership can support an application, subject to the statutory conditions and an individual decision.

Check the operative text, property record and applicant documents before acting.

  1. Does the specific property and ownership share meet Article 56?Why this question: The route is restricted to stated categories and proof of ownership.
  2. Which Article 43 conditions and valuation evidence apply to this applicant?Why this question: Ownership alone is insufficient for approval.
